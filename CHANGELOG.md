# Changelog

One line per user-visible change, newest release first. The format is the one
`docs/maintainers.md` §4 and the `kanso-release` skill require; versions are semver.

## v0.14.1

- **The top-of-book venue applies every quote and print the sleeve is handed.** nautilus_trader 1.231.0's top-of-book matching engine ignored a quote or a print stamped (`ts_event`) before its book's last update, while the sleeve was handed it — measured on 85 sessions each of MSTR and COIN, 12 % of quotes and 53 % of prints with Massive quotes and lit prints — so a resting order a later point went through stayed unfilled and a market order sent on an ignored quote filled against the book before it. A venue module, `kanso.nautilus.availability`, loaded on both code paths after the corporate actions, now empties that book first. No point is copied or re-stamped, so the sleeve is handed every point at the same `data_time` and the stream's digest is the same; what moves is what the venue fills, and with it every order a sleeve sends because of a fill or of the position one leaves. A split's restatement is no longer skipped where a bar of the split name, published after the instant of the point that applied the split and before that point reached the venue, had stamped the book past it, nor where a quote had shown one side of the split name's book at size zero: the side the book shows is restated and the other left empty, where the side it showed used to keep quoting the old share count. With a position open, that one-sided book ended the run with `AttributeError` on 0.14.0, and this release's venue, which applies such a quote even when it is stamped before the book's last update, meets it more often; the fraction a split leaves is now paid at the side the book still quotes, or at the position's last fill when it quotes neither (row 155). `kanso doctor` checks the engine's filter and the reset that undoes it as two more engine facts. Measured on three sessions of each name, a probe resting at the touch filled 2.8–3.2 % more orders and 6.0–7.5 % more maker quantity, an unmeasured share of it the whole-order fills below. **A `best`, a card or a certificate struck before this on a hypothesis that requires `quote` or `trade` and not `book`, or whose universe holds a name with a split beside other names, is not comparable with one after it: end those runs on upgrade and begin each again with `kanso research begin ID --from-workspace`, which clears the hypothesis's `best`.** kanso does not end them, a run begun without the flag is seeded from and judged against the old `best`, and a re-pin that changes nothing in scope keeps it. A paper stage holding a version certified before this on such a hypothesis now trades the new venue and is judged — and can be marked `promotable` — against what its certificate measured on the old one: retire it with `kanso strat retire STRATEGY@V` and certify again. Re-certifying the same bytes under the same file, plan and engine is refused as a repeat; re-pinning a changed hypothesis file with `kanso hyp add` is the way meanwhile (row 153). A print stands as the book until the next quote, and where a command lands while one does the research path matches the resting orders against it and the node does not, so the two paths can fill an order differently while `kanso replay parity`, which compares intents, calls them identical; that is older than this release, which brings into it every print the venue used to skip — on synthetic tapes at 20–30 ms, 54–59 % more fills that differ between the paths (row 154). A tape carrying late-reported prints now moves the venue with them (row 152).
- **`kanso doctor` and the docs say what a point beyond a resting limit fills.** On the top-of-book venue a quote beyond a resting limit, or a print beyond it from the side that can trade with it, fills all that is left of it at its price, whatever its own size — of several orders it goes beyond, the best-priced, and nothing of one resting at a worse price; a quote at its price fills it by the size shown and again at every quote that shows it; a quote at a price, or a print at it under `touch`, is credited whole to every order resting there; and a print is the book until the next quote. `docs/concepts.md` and `docs/workspace.md` said a print fills by its own size and no more, which holds only at the price and only for an order alone there, and the engine claim of that name now says the first. Six engine facts join it: the whole fill, a point beyond two orders filling only the better-priced, two orders at one price each credited a point whole, the quote credited again, the print as the book, and why `liquidity_consumption` stays off; the `hypothesis.yaml` template's `limit_fill` comment names the whole fill. Measured over lit prints and no quotes, 7–36 % of what the venue filled for a resting 445 was beyond what the prints' own sizes allowed (row 151).

## v0.14.0 — 2026-10-08

- **`kanso screen`: measure the relationship a hypothesis rests on before it gets a lane.** `kanso screen new`, `validate`, `run`, `show` and `draft` read `screens/<id>/screen.yaml` — bound to a registered hypothesis's research window and costs, or free with a window of its own — and measure with no model call. Two measures ship in a library of the package's own, which an extension cannot add to: `lead_lag`, correlation at declared lags on a grid or by Hayashi–Yoshida between asynchronous prints, and `response`, what follows a `move_bp` or `z` trigger on one leg at declared horizons after `latency_ms`, set against the hurdle a card would pay — a taker round trip struck with the runner's `fill_cost` on the resolved venue model, booked nowhere. Legs are bars, prints, quotes or a book, or a basket, a spread (fixed or fitted hedge) or a gap derived from them; a lead under a second between legs whose timestamp kinds differ is flagged `clock_bound`. The session is the unit of replication: each measure's cells are judged together by a max-T over session sign flips, `[screen] draws` of them (default 9,999), every vector taken and p exact when they fit, and the optional `verdict` has no defaults. `screen run` fetches what an adapter serves, snapshots it and pins the snapshot; a result is immutable, keyed by the screen's bytes, the snapshot and the measure library's version (the package version and a digest of the library), and stored in `state.db` (migration 0010 — run `kanso migrate` after upgrading). A data adapter, a workspace extension's included, tells a screen what it can fetch through four optional members, `timestamps`, `serves`, `spec_for` and `venues` (`docs/extensions.md`); `massive` declares the first three and `okx` all four. The embargo binds both ways: a free screen whose window meets a registered hypothesis's certification span on a shared instrument is refused at `screen run` (exit 2), and `hyp validate` and `hyp add` refuse a certification window that, with the embargo before it, meets data a recorded screen read for one of the universe's instruments (exit 3). `screen draft ID --cell C --as NEW --certify A..B` writes a `response` cell as a draft hypothesis whose seed `strategy.py` is the cell's rule. The synthetic loader can plant a lead (`leader_seed`, `leader_index`, `lag_steps`, `coupling`), and the demo gains `LAGD.SIM`, which takes 0.6 of `DEMO`'s shock a minute late, and `screens/demo_lag`, which the README runs: the screen reads the lead back as 0.596 at one minute, following a 20 bp `DEMO` move in `LAGD` clears the 5 bp round trip by 9.2 bp an event at 15.7 a day, and the drafted baseline card scores 9.14 bp a trade through the runner. Driven on live OKX prints, a cell's −5.00 bp margin came back from the drafted card as −5.15 bp a trade.
- **Linear crypto perpetuals.** An `instruments.yaml` entry of asset class `CRYPTOCURRENCY` with `instrument_class: swap` in its `override` builds the engine's `CryptoPerpetual`. kanso requires `settlement_currency`, `size_increment`, `multiplier` and `lot_size` — the engine defaults the last two to 1, which would be a claim about the contract — and refuses an inverse contract by name (exit 3), since the runner's notional is qty × price × multiplier. A definition carrying a non-zero `maker_fee` or `taker_fee` is refused, because the simulated venue would charge it on top of the runner's one deduction (zero a provider's rate with `maker_fee: "0"` and `taker_fee: "0"` in the entry's `override`), and `kanso doctor`'s `instruments` check fails on a stored definition that carries one. `hyp validate` refuses (exit 3) any universe instrument settling or booked in a currency other than its venue's account currency (`docs/backlog.md` row 46 closed): an index or signal leg quoted in another currency, accepted before, is now refused. A freshly resolved Massive future is filed as `COMMODITY` rather than `EQUITY`, so its definition checksum differs; entries already in `instruments.yaml` keep their class. Margin and liquidation are not simulated (row 103).
- **`funding`: a perpetual's realised funding, delivered and booked.** A built-in custom type, `kanso.data.types.Funding` — `instrument_id` and `rate`, the realised rate of the period that settled as a fraction of notional, never a venue's predicted rate, with `ts_event` and `ts_init` both the settlement instant — loaded by `csv_parquet`, pinned by snapshots, asked only of a universe's perpetuals and handed to `on_data`. A hypothesis whose universe holds a perpetual and does not require `funding` is refused at `hyp validate` (exit 3). The runner books each settlement once, in the extraction, as qty × mark × multiplier × rate on the signed quantity held — a long pays a positive rate, a short receives it — marked at the last print at or before the instant and inside that period's return; it counts every fill stamped before the instant and none stamped at it, so an order answering the rate the sleeve is handed there neither collects nor escapes it. `Trade.funding` is in `pnl_net` and outside `cost`, so `cost_stress` and `cost_scenario` leave it as booked, and `self.balance` has booked it before `on_data` sees the point. Measured on a manual perpetual held across seven settlements: equity moved by exactly the sum paid at every period end (largest difference 4.3e-12), and `kanso replay parity` was identical at tolerance zero on a sleeve sized from the balance funding moves. A non-finite decimal in any custom type's column is refused at load. A node has no live funding feed (row 107).
- **`calendar: continuous` on the synthetic loader.** Every calendar day is a session, in UTC, 00:00 to 24:00, so bars, quotes and trades print on weekends and `1d` bars are accepted; a continuous spec may also generate `funding` settlements at 00:00, 08:00 and 16:00 UTC, from a seed of their own. A continuous spec stating another `timezone`, `session_start` or `session_end` is refused (exit 3), as is `funding` on a weekday calendar. The default, `weekdays`, is recorded in no manifest, so every existing synthetic dataset and snapshot id is unchanged.
- **The OKX broker package.** `nautilus/adapters/okx/` declares two execution clients, `okx_demo` and `okx`, whose credentials are `KANSO_OKX_DEMO_API_KEY`, `KANSO_OKX_DEMO_API_SECRET` and `KANSO_OKX_DEMO_PASSPHRASE`, and `KANSO_OKX_API_KEY`, `KANSO_OKX_API_SECRET` and `KANSO_OKX_PASSPHRASE` — never the engine's ambient `OKX_*` names, which its clients fall back to when handed none. Both are refused at deploy by the wall-clock rule until the long-running node. `[adapters.okx]` takes `region` — `global`, `eea` or `us`, with no default, since an account's key is accepted only by its own entity's host and the engine's `global` default answers an Australian key as though it did not exist — and `rate_per_second` (default 5). The venue `OKX` is declared a USDT margin account at `commission_bps` 5 and `maker_bps` 2, the exchange's global Regular tier.
- **OKX's public reference and history, with no credential.** With a region stated and `[data] reference = "okx"`, `kanso data instruments resolve BTC-USDT-SWAP.OKX` builds the `CryptoPerpetual` from the exchange's public instruments listing — `ctVal` × `ctMult` as the multiplier, `tickSz`, `lotSz`, `minSz` and `settleCcy` — with zero fees, refusing by name an inverse, a suspended or an unknown contract, or one asked for as of a day before it listed (measured: BTC at multiplier 0.01, tick 0.1, lot 0.01). An id qualified with `.OKX` that nothing has resolved resolves through OKX whatever `[data] reference` names, and every instrument re-resolves through the adapter that resolved it before. Three loaders fill a catalog (a fourth, `okx_book`, is below): `okx_bars` (`history-candles`, re-stamped at the bar's close, the unconfirmed newest row dropped), `okx_trades` (the exchange's daily trade archives, checked by CRC and cached in `catalog/.cache/okx/trades/`, the taker's side as the aggressor) and `okx_funding` (`realizedRate` at `fundingTime`). A range an endpoint cannot serve is refused (exit 3) naming the first day it can, never clamped, and a price or size the definition's precision cannot hold exactly is refused, never rounded; a price an archive spells as a float's full expansion (`0.16186999999999999`) is read as the tick it is. Measured from `us.okx.com` on 2026-09-30: 1 s candles reach back to 2026-03-14 and 1 m candles past 2021, trade archives lag two days and reach back through 2022, and funding history about three months.
- **`okx_bars` keeps a day's pages in flight together, and `rate_per_second` holds every OKX request.** On nautilus_trader 1.231.0 the client's quota holds only a request that names a key, so the table's rate had held the archive listing alone; every API request now goes under one shared key. A day of bars is asked in slices of 300 candles, up to `rate_per_second` at once and never more than 20, and yields the bars a page-by-page walk does. Measured live on a day of BTC-USDT-SWAP 5 s bars (17,280): 25.2 and 23.0 s before, 13.8 and 14.1 s after, the parquet byte-identical. Every OKX API request — the reference and the survey included, not only the loaders — waits out an HTTP 429 or code `50011` — 2, 4, 6 and 8 s — and fails only at the fifth.
- **`okx_book`: the exchange's order-book archive.** Reads a swap's daily 400-level archives — fetched in 64 MiB ranges, read through once before use, and deleted once their day is read — into `OrderBookDelta` changes to the best `levels` of each side (1–400, required), stamped at the exchange's own instant: no `CLEAR` and no snapshot flag, each fifteen-minute snapshot diffed against the rebuilt book, and a level that leaves the best `levels` only because better ones arrived is not deleted, so an order resting there keeps its place in the queue. Each day opens from the book the day before closed with, kept in `catalog/.cache/okx/book/`. Replayed into the engine's `OrderBook`, its top 3 and its top 10 equalled the archive's after every one of a day's 486,916 messages; a day of BTC at ten levels, 10.8 million changes, loaded at a 0.43 GB peak (row 108 closed for the 400-level archives).
- **A loader may declare `chunk_days`, and is written a dataset per chunk.** `okx_trades`, `okx_book`, `massive_trades` and `massive_quotes` declare one day: `data load`, `backfill` and `sync` write each day as its own dataset with its own manifest, streamed to the catalog in batches of 250,000 points, each run on to the end of its last instant so no instant is split across two files. A day of BTC-USDT-SWAP prints that peaked at 1.8 GB written whole loads at 0.41 GB. A failure on a later day leaves the days before it written and is refused naming the `kanso data backfill --loader ID --spec FILE --to DATE` that writes the rest. For every loader, a `--replace` or `--supersedes` write that fails or is interrupted now keeps the dataset it was replacing — its files are linked aside under `catalog/.replaced/` until the new one is recorded — and an empty answer past a series' last served day is not recorded, so a `sync` asks again for days the source has not yet published (row 109 closed for a loader that declares one).
- **`kanso doctor --check-adapters` reads the OKX account's own fee tier.** With `KANSO_OKX_API_KEY`, `KANSO_OKX_API_SECRET` and `KANSO_OKX_PASSPHRASE` set and a region stated, two signed read-only requests read the account's tier and mode and set them against the declaration with any `venues.OKX.costs` rate over it: equal is `ok`; paying more than is charged on either side fails and paying less on both warns, each printing the `venues.OKX.costs` lines to state; a spot-mode account fails. With no key it lists the declaration and sends nothing. Measured on 2026-10-01: an Australian account in futures mode pays 5 bp a maker fill and 7 bp a taker, against the declared 2 and 5, so every cost of a night's research had been 3 bp a maker fill and 2 bp a taker fill too low (row 124).
- **`[research] account` and `currency` reach every venue nothing else declares.** They are a layer of the venue model under a broker's declaration, `venues.<MIC>` and a hypothesis's costs, and carry no cost. A currency is a code of two to eight capitals and digits beginning with a letter (`USDT` validates), and one the engine does not register as fiat or crypto is refused at `hyp validate` (exit 3) and before any venue is funded; a USDT account funds at the engine's precision. A workspace that never set the two keys resolves the same venue model byte for byte; one whose `kanso.toml` states a non-default account or currency sees its venue model change on upgrade, and its card anchors reset.
- **The data registry discovers the data adapters broker packages expose.** A broker package under `nautilus/adapters/<broker>/` may carry its party's public-history loaders and reference provider as an `ADAPTER`, and a packaged data adapter's id wins a collision. `kanso data adapters` and `doctor` no longer report a broker's `[adapters.<id>]` table as configuring what nothing provides (row 102 closed, row 56 half closed), and `ext show` and `doctor` treat a broker-side id as shadowing an extension's.
- **`depth`: a strategy sees the book at the cadence its account is served it.** `depth: {every_ms: N, levels: K}` on a hypothesis that requires `book` leaves the venue every change, and hands `on_order_book_deltas` the top `K` levels a side as they stood at each multiple of `N` ms and `on_quote_tick` level one at every instant the top moved — on a card, a replay and a deployed stage alike. Measured on 2026-10-02: an ordinary OKX account is served a depth snapshot every 100 ms and the top of the book every 10 ms, and the change-by-change book needs the fourth fee tier. `hyp validate` refuses `depth` without `book`, and `depth` beside `quote` (exit 3); `depth` is scope, so changing it clears `best`; under it `msgbus` and the engine's book subscriptions are denied.
- **A card streams its window to the child in sub-day chunks.** The child starts first and is handed the window on its standard input, read an hour at a time when the hypothesis requires prints, quotes or a book and a day at a time otherwise, cut into chunks of at most 250,000 points between instants, the next read only once the last chunk is written: reading overlaps running, nothing of the window is on disk, and the card is the same however the window is cut. The card's time budget and memory watch start with the child, so the budget counts the read. Prints, quotes and book changes are read in the catalog files' order, because the catalog's sorted query left one instant's points in an order that depended on the span read — 79 of 524,932 instants of a day of BTC prints differed read by the hour and read whole. Measured on a day of BTC's book and prints: a fresh child holds about 0.2 GB and 0.66–0.81 KB a point, about 0.4 GB at the cap, and caps from 10,000 points to none gave the identical card.
- **`kanso replay run` and `parity` stream their window in a card's chunks.** Both paths — and so `parity_replay` — read the range through a card's reads and chunks, the node loaded a chunk at a time once the one before has been released and the node has gone quiet. Measured on 2026-10-02: parity over a day of OKX BTC-USDT-SWAP's three-level book and prints (5,668,044 points) was `identical` on 2,556 intents in 874 s at 0.88 GB resident, where a build reading the day whole reached a 6.0 GB footprint and was stopped; two days of GRAM-USDT-SWAP took 148 s at 0.47 GB against 813 s at 0.98 GB. Certification's own runs and a stage node still read their windows whole (row 128).
- **A session records its stream by digest, and parity compares it.** `session.yaml` gains `stream_sha256`, the sha256 of the bytes `stream.jsonl` held, and the file is no longer written. Parity compares the released count and then the digest before any intent, at no tolerance, so two paths fed different points fail even when neither submitted an order, and `parity_replay`'s evidence carries `node_released`, `engine_released`, `node_stream` and `engine_stream`. Measured on 2026-10-02: 8.76 GB of `stream.jsonl` across eleven parity pairs on one workspace, where a session now costs its intents, 15 KB to 13 MB. A session from an earlier kanso compares on its count alone, `replay show` prints its digest as `not recorded`, and its `stream.jsonl` can go: `rm sessions/*/stream.jsonl`.
- **`kanso state prune [--dry-run]`: delete the stored books no run can select again.** A book is kept while an open run carries its pins, or while a hypothesis that is not retired has them on its newest run under the file it is registered under now and the criteria this kanso judges by; the rest — under a re-pinned file, an earlier kanso, a superseded snapshot or a retired hypothesis — goes, and nothing recorded depends on one. It is refused while a daemon runs (exit 2) and holds the daemon's lock throughout; it copies `state.db` whole to `runs/state-<instant>.db` first, refusing unless the disk holds that and twice what is kept, then rewrites the file; `--dry-run` writes nothing and runs beside the daemon. Measured on a copy of a live store: 17,012 books holding 3,025 MB went, 93 stayed, and the file fell from 3,399 MB to 349 MB in 36 s.
- **`data_by_instrument`: a hypothesis asks each instrument for its own types.** A listed instrument is asked only for its non-empty subset of `data_requirements` — by coverage, and for bars, quotes, prints and a book by the runner and by the strategy's subscriptions too, on every path, while a custom type is read and delivered for every name that holds it — so prints of one source can lead quotes of another; an unlisted instrument is asked for every type. `hyp validate` refuses an instrument outside the universe, a type outside `data_requirements`, an empty list, and a required type no instrument is then asked for. `kanso screen draft` writes it whenever a cell's two legs read different types.
- **A daemon lane explores after five stalls on one best by default.** `[research] explore_after_stalls` now defaults to 5, where it was 0, never: once a hypothesis has stalled five times on one best, its lane runs `kanso hyp explore` without being asked — one new draft hypothesis, offered in the inbox as `explored` and registered by nothing — and claims nothing meanwhile, for at most two model attempts of up to 420 s. **Workspaces initialised on 0.8.0–0.13.x state `explore_after_stalls = 0` in `kanso.toml` and keep never: set it to 5 to opt in.** One initialised before 0.8.0, whose file has no key, gets 5.
- **A book's changes of one instrument at one instant are one `OrderBookDeltas`.** The venue applies them whole and matches once, and `on_order_book_deltas` is called once per instrument and instant, where it was handed a half-moved book a change at a time and the venue matched every state in between. A feed is now marked whenever its universe holds more than one name or it requires prints, quotes or a book, so under a `latency_ms` a command due at a lone print or quote lands before that point's handler, as it did for points sharing an instant. A book hypothesis is refused a UTC day that holds a name's market data and none of its book changes. **A `best` struck on a book hypothesis before this is not comparable with a card after it: end book runs on upgrade**; kanso does not end them, and re-pinning an unchanged file keeps the old `best`, since delivery is not part of a hypothesis's scope.
- **A strategy that binds a name its base class owns is refused before it runs.** Binding `self._close` — a harness method since the exit fix below — or `self._last_price`, or any other name `KansoStrategy` or `KansoModifier` defines or sets, its handlers apart, silently replaced the harness's own and crashed at the first exit; measured in one operator workspace, seventeen strategies assigned `self._close`. `strategy_integrity` discards such a card before any backtest, naming the name, the base and the line, with the owned names read from the installed classes; `hyp validate` refuses a workspace `strategy.py` that binds one (exit 3), a baseline refusal says what to rename, and `kanso doctor` gains a `base names` check that warns. The sizing-only refusal of an overridden harness method is subsumed, closing the override edges of rows 83 and 86.
- **Every strategy is refused the clock's second name and every engine request.** `strategy_integrity` denies `_clock` beside `clock`, every `request_*` an engine actor holds — a book's `request_order_book_*`, `request_funding_rates` and `request_aggregated_bars` among them — and `request_instrument` and `request_instruments` with the cache, so a `strategy.py` reaching one is a discard before any backtest.
- **A stage venue is the card's venue.** A simulated stage builds its exchange through the function a card does, from the venue model each version was certified under — `costs.latency_ms`, a level-two book with queue position for a `book` hypothesis, the fill model — where it ran at zero latency on the top of the book. `portfolio deploy` refuses (exit 2), before it writes anything, two versions on one venue certified under different latencies or one with a book and one without, and `promote` refuses before it records an approval when the live stage could not hold the version; the remedy is `kanso strat retire` on one of them, or re-certification. `kanso.nautilus.node` no longer exports `ACCOUNT_TYPES`.
- **The instrument's multiplier is on every notional the sleeve sizes and the run records.** `Fill` and `Trade` carry `multiplier` — a record without it reads as a share's — and their `notional` is qty × price × multiplier; the sleeve's sizing and headroom, the per-share commission (per contract on a multiplied instrument), `full_book`, the stage book, tenure and `cost_scenario` all read it. Every equity's numbers are unchanged; a run struck on a multiplied instrument before this release is re-run before a cost model is re-applied to it.
- **Coverage reads closed days off a calendar.** `kanso.data.closures` holds the US equity closures from 2003-09-10 to 2027-09-06, checked day by day against a vendor's daily record to 2026-09-25 and the exchanges' published closures after, and found by an instrument's asset class whatever its venue is spelled. A chunked equity series' spans join across weekends and holidays, `data backfill` never asks for a closed day, and `research begin` pins with no second pass — measured on 102 chunks of SOXL and SOXS 1 m bars that had merged into about 22 pieces each. A window may begin or end on closed days just past what was served. An empty answer is never coverage, where 0.9.2 to 0.13 counted one between two served spans: `data show` lists it under `empty` inside a gap, and a chunk answered empty on trading days still refuses (row 91 closed). **Futures, currency pairs and indices have no calendar on file, so a chunked backfill of one now breaks at every weekend its chunk edges meet, where a second pass used to close it** (row 112); crypto reads every day as open.
- **Massive: a page that does not answer is asked for again, and a day of ticks is a dataset.** Inside a cursor walk, a page that timed out, lost its connection, met a server error or came back unreadable is asked for twice more, after 2 s and 8 s, before the walk fails; a throttle never is. `[adapters.massive] timeout_s` defaults to 120 s (was 30), and `massive_trades` and `massive_quotes` declare `chunk_days` 1. Measured on 2026-10-05: three walks of ten sessions of MSTR prints were each lost to one page timing out.
- **A stall's certification, a benchmark's hold and a monitor's demotion run in children.** A lane certifies a stall in a child held to its share of memory and killed on a stop, recording `cert_peak_mem_gb` and `cert_wall_s` on the `stalled` event; a certification that needs more is refused naming `[env] mem_per_lane_gb` and `kanso cert run`, which still certifies in your own process. The hold a benchmark objective differences against runs in a child streamed as a card is, and one that will not run refuses the run before the baseline with its reason. The monitor demotes in a child a stop leaves to finish. Measured on a 16 GB host with six lanes at 2.5 GB: lanes that had certified in their own process held 2.9 and 4.1 GB idle and swap reached 13.3 GB; a lane's own peak now moves by about 1 MB over a certification (about 40 MB in-process), 0.1–0.2 MB over a hold (7.6 MB), and the monitor's by about 1 MB over a demotion (35.2 MB) (rows 119, 121 and 122 closed).
- **A run reads its instrument definitions from the store.** A run's start and every card build the venue model from the definitions the store holds — the ones its snapshot pins — and ask the reference adapter only about an id the store holds none of; before, every card of every lane asked the vendor once per instrument whenever the store's resolution was dated other than the research start. Measured on 2026-10-01: five lanes starting together on the OKX reference lost four to an HTTP 429 in the following second, each put back in the queue at priority −2. `hyp validate` and `hyp add` still ask (row 117).
- **An exit never goes past flat, counting the exits still working.** `submit_exit` sized against the filled position alone, so under a `costs.latency_ms`, and on any broker, a strategy that cancelled its resting exit and sent a new one could have both fill: measured on two book lanes at 20 ms, every card held a position 0.87 to 0.997 of a day against 17 s to 2 min at 0 ms, and one session bought 50 shares, sold 92 and ended 42 short. ## v0.1.2 — 2026-09-07

- The parameter map `kanso classify` and `kanso cert plan` ask a model for is a shape a provider will accept. Both declared theirs as a free-form `{"type": "object"}`, and the Anthropic messages API refuses that document outright — `400 output_config.format.schema: For 'object' type, 'additionalProperties' must be explicitly set to false`, measured on 2026-09-07, one request per candidate shape. A gate's and a construct's parameters now travel as a list of `{name, value}` pairs, the one shape measured both to be accepted **and** to come back filled in; the obvious repair, a closed object with no properties, is accepted and answers `params: {}`, dropping every parameter the model chose. kanso reads the pairs back into a mapping before any step sees them, so a classification, a plan and every gate hold what they always held.
- What that cost a workspace on the `anthropic` protocol: `classify` and `cert plan` are the first two steps of the pipeline, and both were refused on every call, in every workspace, for the life of 0.1.1. What was measured then is the parameter shape; that it was the *only* thing wrong with those two documents was an inference from a refusal that named only it, and sending the corrected documents disproved it — the next entry is what came back. `propose` and `align_check` carry no parameter map and answered 200 before and after. The `mock` protocol, which is what the demo and the suite drive, never had the problem — which is why nothing offline saw it.
- The same shape is admissible on the `openai_compat` wire, whose structured outputs impose the `additionalProperties: false` rule too. That is read from the protocol, not measured: no request from that client has been sent to a server that speaks it. It has a second requirement — every property listed in `required` — which `classify` does not meet, and `docs/backlog.md` row 57 holds that half open.
- A model that names one parameter twice is asked again with the name in the complaint, rather than kanso deciding which of the two values it meant. The retry is the router's ordinary ladder; nothing is repaired silently.
- `mock/responses.yaml` writes every `params` as that same list of pairs, because it is what a real model answers with. A hand-written script that still uses a map is rejected as a wrong answer by the schema check and takes the ladder, so the fix is to rewrite the entry rather than to debug the refusal.
- The `kanso classify` schema asks for its keep rule without a numeric bound, because a numeric bound is a keyword the provider refuses. With the parameter map corrected, `certify_plan`, `propose` and `align_check` were answered 200 with the schemas as shipped and `classify` was answered 400 a second time — `output_config.format.schema: For 'number' type, property 'minimum' is not supported` — for the `minimum: 0` on `min_delta` and `k_se`. Measured on 2026-09-07, one request per keyword: `minimum` and `exclusiveMinimum` are refused on `number` and on `integer` alike, while `minLength`, `maxLength`, `enum`, `pattern`, `minItems` and `description` are all accepted. Both bounds are off the wire, stated in the instruction instead, and enforced where they always were.
- A keep rule outside those bounds is refused by name. A model answering `k_se: 0` is now told `objective.params.k_se: Input should be greater than 0` and asked again. It was already told which field and which bound — `the answer is not a classification: k_se: Input should be greater than 0` — and what changed is that the complaint now locates the field in the answer document, the shape a value outside the toolbox's range already earned, and gives each field its own line rather than one joined sentence. The wire no longer states that bound at all, which makes what the refusal says the whole of what the next attempt has to go on.
- What kanso may send and what kanso can check are two different sets, and only one of them is a property of this package. `models/jsonschema.py` declares `ADMISSIBLE` — the keywords a provider was measured to accept, with the measurement and its date written beside them — next to the `VOCABULARY` its own checker reads, and the suite now holds every node of every shipped schema to each set separately. Neither contains the other, which is why the guard that shipped with the entries above could not have caught this: `minimum` is in the vocabulary.
- Docs: `docs/workspace.md` says what shape a scripted `params` takes, in both places it describes `mock/responses.yaml`.
